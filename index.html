Course
2030AFE Business Intelligence · 2026
My role
Sole analyst: data cleaning, model, DAX, dashboard and report
Data
Retail orders, 2014 to 2017 · 9,994 order lines · 5,009 orders · 793 customers

The question

Sales rose from $484K in 2014 to $733K in 2017, but profit margin stayed around 12 to 13% and slipped in 2017. The sales director asked where profit was being lost, why, and what could fix it without hurting growth.

What I did

  1. Cleaned the source workbook in Power Query: removed header rows, resolved duplicate postal codes and product IDs, trimmed names and fixed data types
  2. Modelled a star schema with one sales fact table and five dimensions (customer, region, product, order, date)
  3. Wrote DAX measures for sales, profit, margin, average discount and loss amount, plus a discount-band column
  4. Worked from what happened, to why, to what to do, cross-checking each finding with Year, Region, Category, Segment and Ship Mode slicers
  5. Turned the findings into five decisions with evidence, expected impact and a monitoring plan

Limits

  • No freight or warehousing costs in the data, so true product margin is unknown
  • The scenario assumes retained sales are unaffected by removing deep discounts

If I did it again

  • Pilot the 20% cap in Texas and Illinois for a quarter and compare against other regions
  • Add cost and discount-reason fields to the model

Comments and replies from nine official music videos · 12,000 rows · 9,581 unique accounts

The question

What are fans saying about Taylor Swift, Billie Eilish and Olivia Rodrigo, how do those conversations differ, and what can a label do with that knowledge?

What I did

  2. Collected whole comment threads to 4,000 rows per artist so no reply lost its parent comment, and cached raw data so re-runs don't call the API

Sole analyst
Data
Study hours and marks · 12 students · 2 variables

The question

Twelve students logged weekly study hours and their marks. Is there a relationship, and what mark would we expect from 6.7 hours a week?

What I did

  1. Wrote a describe_data() function for mean, median and standard deviation
  2. Compared the two variables with box plots, then a scatter plot
  3. Fitted a least-squares line with np.polyfit and drew it over the points

  • Add a confidence interval around the prediction
